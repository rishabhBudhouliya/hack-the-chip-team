# RHS cache experiment on Trainium2

Shape: M=512, K=256, N=1024, float32; LNC=1.

| Metric | Original reference | RHS cached |
|---|---:|---:|
| Compiler prediction | 65.94 us | 56.50 us |
| Profiled device median | 160.312 us | 142.798 us |
| Simulator HBM traffic | 7 MiB | 4 MiB |
| Simulator DMA transfers | 40 | 28 |

The cached kernel passed all four lab simulation shapes. The hardware benchmark verified numerical output at the measured shape. After excluding one validation execution and ten warmups per kernel, the candidate was faster in all 20 timed pairs, reducing device median by 10.92%. HBM traffic fell 42.86% and remains 14.29% above the assumed minimum.

All 62 ExecutionInfo durations match nc_exec_running within 1 ns and model IDs match. Mapping follows the benchmark first executing reference then candidate; NEFF paths contain reference/file.neff and candidate/file.neff.

Earlier captures measured the original at about 129 us; this capture measured it at about 160 us. The present within-capture comparison supports an improvement, but absolute times should not be compared across captures to claim superiority over the earlier allocation-only candidate. Cause of the baseline shift is unverified. Repeat this comparison in independent fresh processes before reporting repeatability. Profiling may affect absolute durations.

This cached kernel was explicitly implemented by the assistant based on the roofline diagnosis; it was not generated by the model optimization loop. The kernel bounds K to 512 for this cache experiment.

## Timing shift within the capture

| Timed iterations | Reference median | Cached median | Median reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| First 10 | 129.607 us | 112.745 us | 13.01% |
| Last 10 | 189.010 us | 172.567 us | 8.70% |

Median saving across paired iterations: 16.747 us. Both kernels stepped up by roughly 60 us after the tenth timed iteration. The cause is unverified; contention or a device state change are possible explanations, not established facts. The overall 10.92% median reduction mixes these two timing regimes; the split comparison better describes this capture.


## Independent repeats

| Capture | Reference median | Cached median | Reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| First capture | 160.312 us | 142.798 us | 10.92% |
| Repeat 1 | 129.270 us | 112.686 us | 12.83% |
| Repeat 2 | 129.204 us | 112.745 us | 12.74% |

Both repeats have stable timings: reference about 129 us and cached about 113 us, with no roughly 60 us step like the first capture. The candidate was faster in all 60 paired timed iterations across three fresh processes. The two stable repeats show 12.74–12.83% lower profiled device median latency. HBM traffic remains reduced from 7 MiB to 4 MiB (42.86%). Numerical correctness at the benchmark shape was verified by the hardware benchmark, and all four lab shapes passed simulation. Device/runtime duration crosschecks agree within 1 ns for all 186 events.

Repeatable result under the tested conditions: explicit RHS caching reduces profiled device median latency by about 12.8% in the two stable repeat captures. The first capture also favors caching, but its overall median mixes two timing regimes. This is not an autonomous model-generated cache optimization; it was implemented by the assistant from the roofline diagnosis.
